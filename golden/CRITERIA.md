## Gold answer writing criteria (decided by Laura during labeling)

- Gold answers are complete sentences, never bare noun lists.
- Paraphrasing is allowed for readability, but meaning must stay faithful to the cited chunk, and every claim must be supported by the citation. Simplifying must not drop content.
- Plain, easy-to-read sentences are preferred over long, dense ones.
- Lists: an intro sentence, then one item per line with a blank line between items.
- Framework labels (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE and their numbers) are always in bold.
- Items use parallel structure, without repeating the same subject on every line.
- Subcategory numbers are only given when the corpus maps them explicitly (see g15).
- Plain-language principle (Laura): we adapt very technical NIST language into short, simple sentences that most people can read. We accept a slightly longer reading time in exchange for better comprehension: communication comes first.

### Plain-language principle

Gold answers adapt very technical NIST language into short, simple sentences that most people can read. This matters because many adults struggle with dense text: in the 2023 U.S. adult skills survey, 28% of adults aged 16–65 scored at the lowest literacy level ([NCES, PIAAC 2023](https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/piaac/2023/national_results.asp)). Federal plain-language guidance recommends one idea per sentence, because shorter sentences "break the information up into smaller, easier-to-process units" ([Federal Plain Language Guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GSA/plainlanguage.gov/main/_pages/guidelines/concise/write-short-sentences.md)). Health agencies give similar advice and note that text written for the "average" adult is still hard for about half of adults ([AHRQ, Tool 11](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool11.html)). Simpler sentences can make an answer longer and slower to read. We accept that cost, because the goal is for the reader to understand the answer.

### Additional rules
- Lists whose items have no label (no GOVERN/MAP/... number) use bullet points, one per line.
- Acronyms are spelled out once per answer, at first mention. "AI" is never spelled out, not even inside another expansion (e.g. "AI RMF (AI Risk Management Framework)").
- No vague references such as "the framework covers this": always name the topic explicitly.
- When an answer names several categories and explains one, list all categories as bullets first, then explain the relevant one in its own paragraph.
- Citations are not written in the answer text; they live in `gold_citations`.
- Unanswerable cases use one standard sentence: "The NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology) AI RMF (AI Risk Management Framework) documents provided do not contain this information."
- False-premise answers start with "The question starts from a false idea."
- Every new rule is applied retroactively to all already-reviewed cases.
